Most of my research asks the same question: does the model actually know what it seems to know? So far that has meant LLMs answering questions about amended Thai law, and a genomics model that barely reads its own input.

Accepted NeurIPS 2026, New in ML and Global South AI workshops

Still Citing the Old Law? Statutory Knowledge and Temporal Anchoring in Thai Legal Question Answering

When a law is amended, does an LLM answer with the current version or the repealed one, and can you tell the difference between that and simply not knowing? We built a benchmark to separate the two. Across nine LLMs, 97.7% of the wrong answers came with no sign of uncertainty.

Key findings
  • A bilingual Thai/English benchmark of 60 vignettes on four dated amendments to Thai law, with every gold label traceable to the Royal Gazette.
  • Nine LLMs evaluated over 3,036 responses, separating anchoring to repealed law from lack of knowledge.
  • 97.7% of the wrong answers came with no expression of uncertainty.

Accepted NeurIPS 2026, New in ML and ICBINB workshops

Any Promoter Will Do: Auditing a Sequence-to-Expression Model on Patient-Derived Data

A released convolutional model claims to predict mRNA abundance from promoter sequence. We ran it on patient exome data and added the baselines and ablations it was missing, to check how much it really uses the sequence. Barely: swapping, shuffling or scrambling the promoter changes R² by under 0.010.

Key findings
  • Audited on patient exome data covering 15,370 genes.
  • Added the missing baselines and ablations.
  • Swapping, shuffling or scrambling the promoter changes R² by under 0.010: the model barely uses the sequence it is given.
